# 7. Conclusion

This thesis examined the association between water and sanitation conditions and child anthropometric outcomes in four Sub-Saharan African countries — Ethiopia, Ghana, Kenya, and Nigeria — distinguishing throughout between a child's own household facilities and the WASH conditions of the surrounding community, and between a single survey-date exposure measurement and a preliminary, geospatially matched measure of local WASH coverage during a child's own early-life window.

The baseline evidence does not support a clear, robust association between improved household WASH and child height-for-age, weight-for-age, or weight-for-height once comparisons are restricted to households within the same DHS cluster: none of the six household cluster-fixed-effects coefficients across the three outcomes and two exposures reaches conventional significance under this thesis's primary inference convention. Community-level WASH coverage shows a large, strongly significant association before adjustment, but this attenuates to statistical insignificance once socioeconomic status and region fixed effects are added — a pattern consistent with confounding by local development, though this design cannot establish confounding as the only explanation, and this thesis does not claim otherwise. These findings sit consistently alongside, rather than in contradiction to, the strongest available causal evidence on WASH interventions [@Cumming2019], which similarly finds no detectable effect of basic, household-level WASH interventions on linear growth, while being explicit that this does not challenge WASH's biological plausibility and that a facility-type classification (this thesis's own exposure) is not the same as a comprehensive intervention package (Section 2.3), so this parallel is suggestive rather than confirmatory.

The preliminary historical extension — linking three countries' earlier DHS rounds to modelled annual local WASH coverage for a defined early-life window — produces one result that stands out from the rest of this thesis's findings: a positive, statistically significant pooled association between local water coverage and height-for-age (β = 1.03 per 10 percentage points, 95% CI [0.51, 1.54], *p* < .001), independently reconstructed and confirmed computationally during this project. This result is reported with the full set of qualifications developed in Section 6: it is sensitive to the pooled weighting convention (falling to β = 0.41, 95% CI [0.03, 0.80], *p* = .035, unweighted on the identical 23,018 observations), rests on an undocumented measurement-subsample sampling weight, reflects area-averaged rather than population-weighted exposure around displaced cluster coordinates, and cannot be cleanly attributed to exposure timing alone because the baseline and historical samples differ in several respects simultaneously. It is a promising, carefully qualified preliminary finding, not a settled result, and this thesis does not use it to overturn or reinterpret the baseline's more cautious conclusions.

Taken together, this thesis's contribution is a transparent, side-by-side comparison of household and community WASH exposure within one consistent four-country framework, together with a bounded, honestly limited first attempt to introduce genuine temporal exposure variation into that comparison. Neither the baseline nor the historical results, individually or together, establish a causal effect of WASH facilities on child nutritional outcomes in these four countries, and neither should be read as evidence for or against the value of WASH investment as a development policy — a question this associational, four-country design was never positioned to answer. What this thesis does offer is a careful accounting of where the available evidence is strong, where it is weak, and where — particularly in the historical extension's unresolved measurement documentation and the baseline's inherent exposure-timing limitation — further data or a different design would be needed before a stronger claim could responsibly be made. Future work extending the historical approach to all four countries, resolving the measurement-subsample weighting question through direct engagement with DHS methodological documentation, and pairing geospatial WASH matching with a genuinely panel or repeated-cross-section design, would be a natural next step toward narrowing the gap this thesis has tried to describe honestly rather than close.
